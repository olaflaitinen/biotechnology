## Acknowledgements

### Sources of the taxonomy

The colour-coded classification of biotechnology has no single author. It
emerged from European science-policy and science-communication writing from the
late 1990s onward, initially as a red/green contrast during the public debate
over genetically modified food, and was extended colour by colour as other
application areas needed names. This library records that convention; it does
not originate it. See [`FAQ.md`](FAQ.md) for what is known about the origin of
each colour.

### Bodies whose published work the content rests on

Cited by identifier throughout, and gratefully acknowledged:

- **European Commission** and **EUR-Lex**, for the open publication of Union
  law, and for the EUPL itself.
- **European Medicines Agency**, **European Food Safety Authority** and
  **European Chemicals Agency**, for published guidance.
- **World Health Organization**, **World Organisation for Animal Health**,
  **Food and Agriculture Organization**, for technical report series and
  manuals that are freely available worldwide.
- **International Council for Harmonisation**, **OECD**, **EPPO**, **UPOV**,
  for guidance documents.
- **United Nations**, for the 2030 Agenda and the Sustainable Development Goals
  referenced by every record.
- **Implementing bodies of the Biological Weapons Convention**, whose open
  literature is the basis of the defensive framing of the `dark` branch.

### Methodological influences

- The **FAIR data principles** (Wilkinson et al., 2016), which shaped the
  export formats, the stable identifiers and the decision to validate every
  cross-reference mechanically.
- **Keep a Changelog** and **Semantic Versioning**, for release discipline.
- The **Contributor Covenant**, from which `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` is adapted.
- The **Citation File Format**, which `CITATION.cff` conforms to.
- The plain-language movement in public administration, which is the reason
  every record in this library is written twice rather than once.
